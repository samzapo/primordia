# Living cell architecture

## Authoritative state

`simulation.gd` advances at 60 Hz, independent of scenes, rendering, and input. `cell.gd` supplies the same anatomy, material accounting, damage, repair, volume, and inertia for the player and NPCs. Initial roles select anatomy; they do not supply invulnerability or a separate pursuit controller. The seeded RNG and stable array order support replay on one engine/platform.

The world is a 4096 × 2560 torus. `medium.gd` defines nearest-image distances and wrapping; the spatial grid wraps its buckets too. Rendering projects the nearest image around the player's cell, so the following camera never jumps at a seam. The visible center advances fully, the elliptical fringe slows smoothly, and positions outside the ellipse do not advance. Dormant fluid nodes cannot exchange impulses with active neighbors. Death/replacement events are recorded separately; replacement is explicit external ecosystem input.

A tick updates the active fluid, transports locally queried active particles and updates their spatial buckets, processes chemistry and cell biology, updates anatomy, applies three motion/contact substeps, then feeding between cells and toxin contact. Death removes individuals once. The spatial grid persists between ticks; crossing a bucket updates only that particle. Dead packets are compacted and the grid rebuilt every half-second before replenishment. Its indices belong to that particle array. Local rectangular queries visit each wrapped bucket at most once, including when zoom exposes the entire map.

## Cell material and geometry

Organic material is tracked in structural body mass, available biomass, organs, gut substrate, and pending waste. Assembly transfers reserves into organs. Damage releases their lost material; repair incorporates reserves. Recycling returns half the remaining material and releases the other half as waste. A puncture drains reserves, then structure, and emits nutrient material at the wound. Local leakage packets merge only with active neighbors to keep continuous leakage from generating excessive tiny particles.

Effective volume is `body + biomass`. Render radius follows a sphere-inspired cube root of that volume, projected into 2D. Capacity uses the actual 2D membrane perimeter and each organ's arc footprint plus clearance. It is not a full 3D surface-packing model. Each organ stores a fixed material coordinate interpolated between two membrane nodes. Translation, rotation, growth, and deformation move its attachment point, while liquid flow does not slide it along the membrane. New placement checks physical arc clearance. Shrinkage sheds crowded organs as accounted-for debris.

Translational mass includes retained organic material. Rotational inertia includes distributed cytoplasm and organ mass at its attachment radius. Propulsion at asymmetric locations produces torque. There is no historical-food-only growth counter; losing retained mass reduces size. Radius has no arbitrary upper cap. Gut, oxygen and ATP storage, digestion throughput and baseline ATP maintenance scale with effective surface area, `(volume / 78)^(2/3)` (minimum scale 1). This removes the previous fixed gut/ATP bottleneck. Food supply, oxygen, anatomy, inertia and damage still limit practical growth. The finite toroidal habitat remains a numerical/world-size limit; unbounded mathematical size is not a claim that arbitrarily huge overlapping copies can be simulated faithfully.

`membrane.gd` solves 28 boundary nodes with compliant XPBD edge and enclosed-area constraints. Four internal tensile chords and elliptical rest geometry support noncircular shapes while preserving pressure-driven area. The art displays the actual chords. This remains a stabilized 2D membrane model, not a microtubule or cytoskeleton material simulation.

Contacts use radial extents sampled from the membrane. Sweeps prevent a moving body from crossing another between steps; iterative mass-weighted overlap and velocity impulses separate compressed bodies. Contact locally compresses membrane nodes. This is a hybrid body/ring model, not a full deformable contact manifold. Deep initial overlap has no unique physical history; the solver selects a consistent separation normal.

## Finite-mass liquid

The medium has 64 × 40 velocity nodes, spaced 64 world units apart. Each has 22 mass units, comparable to the material surrounding a starting cell of about 100 units. Bilinear sampling transports chemicals. Six cell boundary points exchange implicit drag impulses with active fluid nodes. Each body impulse has an equal and opposite fluid impulse, including reaction to propulsion. Rotational velocity participates in drag. Undriven viscosity exchanges velocity symmetrically, conserving linear momentum.

Periodic currents initialize local eddies. A weak relaxation toward that field is an explicit external tide, a momentum/energy source and sink. Dormant nodes retain their velocity. The numerical tests disable forcing to measure conservation and dissipative drag independently. Bilinear samples avoid temporary stencil arrays, the tide field is cached, and camera activity enumerates only relevant nodes; the mass and exchange laws are unchanged.

This is a mass-lumped viscous medium with fixed density, not an incompressible fluid solver: no pressure projection, moving free surface, or volume-exclusion calculation is claimed. It provides substantial two-way motion coupling. Fluid traction does not yet solve membrane shape jointly with contact and pressure.

## Chemical and sensory particles

Five external species are authoritative: nutrient, oxygen, waste, cell trace, and toxin. Actual local particle contact triggers absorption, decomposition, or damage; rendered particles are not a substitute for the simulation.

In game units, respiration consumes one food and one oxygen unit, producing five ATP, 0.8 biomass, and 0.2 waste. Waste decomposition requires nearby oxygen and yields 0.6 food per waste unit; the remaining 0.4 leaves the organic pool as a recorded respiration loss. Photosynthetic habitat input explicitly replenishes local food and oxygen. Recycling cannot create an infinite resource loop. These are coarse resource rules, not molecular stoichiometry.

Receptors detect food or cell trace particles. Nutrient receptors approach food; hunting receptors approach cells whose radius is at most the eater’s radius divided by 1.15; host receptors approach cells over 5% larger; flight receptors avoid other living cells. Only an installed flight receptor authorizes flight, and removal cancels its queued/remembered signals. Flight has priority over pursuit. Engulfing/proboscis mouths have 150-unit local detection; dedicated receptors reach 310 units from their attachment. Sampling occurs every 0.15 seconds, and signal particles travel inward before updating a remembered target position for up to 1.2 seconds. Dead trace owners are rejected. Placement orients a suitable mouth toward that target; propulsion supplies the actual forces. No direct steering or behavior wiring editor is introduced.

The feeding category has three modes. Grazers absorb nutrient packets, including carcasses. Engulfers damage sufficiently smaller cells through mouth contact and transfer a gut-capacity-limited portion upon death, releasing the remainder. Proboscises puncture local defenses and drain larger living hosts only once a hole exists at the contact point. A remote hole cannot bypass armor there. Feeding contacts damp relative velocity with equal/opposite impulses. The renderer shows the active link, moving food packets and engulfing progress; text explains unsuitable size, full gut, local armor and draining. All food enters the same metabolism and milestone counter. Organelle categories remain unchanged.

Damage is local. An organ at impact absorbs damage first. Exposed membrane can develop a leaking hole even when other organs survive. Baseline closure is quick; membrane and organ restoration use reserves and ATP, and regenerative organs accelerate both. Lethal damage cannot be undone by the healing phase. A hunter's death releases retained organic material once; replacement has a fresh identity beyond view and its initial material is logged as ecosystem input. When zoom leaves no hidden space for a whole organism, replacement is deferred until such space exists.

## Rendering, input, and limits

`pond.gd` projects the full viewport around the player. Native Controls draw an overlay HUD, scrollable assembly and settings, with persistent 85–175% font/HUD scaling and fullscreen. Tab handles input before GUI focus navigation, pauses assembly and restores the prior manual-pause state when closed. Settings also pause. F11 toggles fullscreen. F3 reports measured FPS, simulation time and frame preparation time (not GPU duration).

`cell_view.gd` caches local procedural drawing until a simulation revision changes it, while native node transforms interpolate translation/rotation each rendered frame. `membrane.gd` caches its outline and cumulative arc lengths after deformation. Five MultiMeshes receive bulk buffers for visible particles, beginning at 256 instances per species and growing as needed. Only local buckets are scanned for rendering. Particle and cell interpolation use nearest-image toroidal displacement, avoiding seam jumps. Current marks refresh at 15 Hz and retain world positions between refreshes. None of these optimizations changes fluid mass or the coupling law.

The viewport and microscope zoom determine the active ellipse; zooming out increases active work. Automatic camera pullback keeps a growing player visible, with manual wheel zoom available. The vignette darkens the activity fringe. Assembly projects clicks onto the deformed membrane rather than sockets.

Tests cover conservation, local armor, explicit flight, mouth pursuit and receptor reach, growing metabolism, anchored anatomy, spatial updates, seam interpolation, dormancy, pressure/tension stability, compressed contacts, death/replacement, deterministic replay, autonomous diets and GUI input/preferences. Captures and native fullscreen transitions are inspected separately. Launchers fail on script errors even if Godot exits zero. `tests/profile.gd` records CPU tick percentiles and phase costs; F3 in actual play includes the frame rate. See [revision three](REVISION_3.md) for validation evidence and remaining limits.
